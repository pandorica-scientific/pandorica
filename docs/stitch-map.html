Pandorica stitch map

The stitcher joins a stack of serial-section electron tomograms into one volume and one microtubule network. The images of each cut set every section's pose: rotation, shift and the knife's x/y stretch. The microtubule ends that cross each cut then bend the rest into place, with a warp that may neither fold nor swirl. Joints it cannot vouch for are flagged.

  • microtubule lengths in ρ (end spacing)
  • warps checked: det J ≥ 0.05, |curl| ≤ 0.3
  • no landmarks by hand
  • CPU, CUDA or Apple MPS
  • Amira files in, Amira files out

Snapshot of 2026-10-15: the stitcher as it is in the code (commit bc7b222, pandorica 1.5.0; the stitcher code last changed in b143e42 on 2026-06-19). Settings come from run_stitch (pandorica/stitch/cli.py) and the functions it calls; results come from HISTORY.md, the code's own notes and the stitcher review of 2026-09-23 and 24. Plain-language tour: HOW_IT_WORKS.md; reference: pandorica/stitch/README.md.

1 · Pipeline

The pipeline

Code: run_stitch in pandorica/stitch/cli.py, then stitch_sections in pandorica/stitch/pipeline/stitcher.py. The default path, when every section has a volume and a microtubule graph. Read the top lane (the image) and the lane below it (the microtubules) left to right; numbers give the order in which the steps run.

How data moves through it

  1. Load. The folder holds one image (<name>_secNN.am) and one graph (<name>_secNN_spatialGraph.am) per section, paired by name. The graphs load at once; each volume is read when a step needs it and dropped after. When every section has both, the path drawn above runs; section 4 covers the other cases.
  2. Read the faces. For the image: the maximum over the 10 outermost slices of each face, read at half resolution in all three axes (5 slices). For the microtubules: every microtubule whose end lies in the outer 15% of the section's Z range, with a direction from its last 20% of points (at least 3). ρ is the median nearest-neighbour distance between the ends on section k's top face; the microtubule thresholds are multiples of it.
  3. Image coarse pose. For each interface, the rotation that most image cells agree on, the shift, and, when it exceeds 2%, an x/y stretch. The relative poses compose into one absolute pose per section, with section 0 fixed. Interfaces the image cannot settle are marked for review in the log.
  4. Match the ends. The same relative pose moves section k+1's bottom ends onto section k's top ends; the matcher pairs them one to one, and a bootstrap recovers pairs that one pass misses.
  5. Guarded warp. A thin-plate spline through the pairs bends section k+1's face onto section k's. It is kept only if it neither folds nor swirls; otherwise it is smoothed more, and in the end rejected.
  6. QC certificate. Each interface is accepted or flagged with its reasons, and marked chainable when its matches alone are good enough to link the microtubules.
  7. Rescue and scale gate. Where the first-pass match is under 30%, the microtubules re-estimate the rotation (adopted only if their match reaches 30% and beats the image's). Where the image committed a stretch, it is dropped if rotation alone matches the microtubules at least 10 points better. Only these interfaces are matched and warped again.
  8. Image fill. Where there are no microtubules, often in the corners, image patches give a second guarded warp per interface, held at zero on the microtubule ends.
  9. Export. Every section is resampled into one canvas with its pose. Each interface's warp (the MT warp plus the fill warp) is split in half between its two faces and blended linearly through Z. The graph gets the same pose and warp; microtubules are joined across chainable interfaces into single filaments and split where the two sides' overall directions differ by more than 45°.

Why two stages? A cut section is distorted in two ways (HOW_IT_WORKS.md). The diamond knife compresses the whole section the same way, which one x/y stretch can undo: the coarse pose. The electron beam then shrinks the plastic unevenly, which differs from spot to spot: the fine warp. The image shows the whole section at once, so it sets the coarse pose. The microtubule ends are dense survey points where they exist, so they set the fine warp; fitting a pose from them instead would force a spatially varying shrink into one global transform (register_warps_to_coarse in pipeline/core.py).

2 · Rationale

Why we think it works

Each design choice, the reason behind it, and how well it is supported today. Measured: shown on a real stack. Tested: shown by a synthetic test. Hint: signs it helps, but not isolated or without ground truth. Open: no evidence yet, or evidence against. Every test is synthetic except three smoke tests on FemalePN, which check that the pipeline runs, not that it is right. No full run has yet been compared with ground truth.

The image sets the whole pose

The knife's compression is the same everywhere in a section, and the image shows the whole section, so the image fixes rotation, shift and the x/y stretch at once. The microtubules do not carry that global stretch, and a pose fitted to their ends would absorb the uneven shrink that belongs in the warp. The stretch is fitted only after the angle is fixed, with a small window, and clamped to a physical range.

Hint

Synthetic: a planted 1.12 × 0.90 stretch is recovered within 0.04, a pure rotation gives no spurious stretch (under 2%), and a spatially varying residual goes to the warp, not the pose (tests/test_image_pose_affine.py, tests/test_warps_to_coarse.py). The code notes 0.07% spurious stretch over a real 10-section chain (image_pose.py). On a real chiral stack, scaling the windows with the face raised the microtubule match from about 26–43% to 60–68% (HISTORY.md). The image stretch can also overfit, which is why the scale gate exists (row below). Not yet compared with known poses.

Rotation by consensus, not by shape

Every angle is tried. At each, the faces are cut into cells, matched, and a rigid move is fitted by RANSAC; the angle whose consistent cells carry the most confidence wins. A wrong 180° flip leaves few cells that agree on one rigid move, so it loses even on a symmetric section. A near-tie between the two branches is flagged rather than trusted.

Hint

Synthetic: the true branch wins on support, and RANSAC recovers an 18° rotation through 9 gross outliers in 24 pairs (tests/test_napari_stitch.py). The contour cross-check reads the angle within 5–13° of the microtubule answer on all four FemalePN interfaces, where an older image search was off by 120–170° (contour_rotation.py, README.md). Against the claim: on a near-circular section with no nuclear contour the image coarse can still give a grossly wrong rotation, for example −140° (HISTORY.md); the microtubule rescue exists for that case. No real-data accuracy for the sweep itself.

The microtubules bend only what is left

The ends are matched in the frame the image pose already set, and no pose is fitted from them. A single match pass drops true partners that the uneven shrink left 2 to 3 ρ apart, because the gates expect them within about 1 ρ. The bootstrap fits a guarded warp on the pairs it has, moves the ends by it and matches again with the same gates, so true partners come within reach while a false neighbour is not pulled along coherently.

Measured

On a dense stack the bootstrap lifted the two weak interfaces from 35% to 49% and from 42% to 63% matched, while every healthy interface held or improved (HISTORY.md). The September review found that it mostly keeps the first pass's pairs: 92–97% survive to the second pass on 3 Monopoles interfaces (section 5).

Clean pairs before any warp

Reading the earlier Amira tool's code pointed to duplicate and clustered pairs as what made its warps spin into whirlpools. So the matcher drops near-duplicate ends, gates every pair by distance, angle and fold-back, takes a one-to-one assignment, uncrosses X-crossed neighbours, checks that each pair could be one continuing fibre, and removes pairs that do not fit one rigid move, all before the warp sees them.

Tested

Synthetic tests of each step: outlier rejection, deduplication, the direction cost, the fold-back gate and the distance gate (tests/test_matcher.py). On real data only match fractions exist, without ground truth. The review found that the assignment first maximises the number of pairs, which reroutes about 5% of them (section 5).

A warp that cannot fold or swirl

Every warp is sampled on a grid and kept only if its Jacobian determinant stays at or above 0.05 (no fold) and its curl stays within 0.3 (no swirl). A swirl can keep det J positive, so both are checked. A warp that fails is smoothed more and refitted; if no smoothing on the ladder passes, it is rejected and the joint keeps its coarse pose. A bad warp is never applied.

Tested

Synthetic: a moderate swirl passes the det J check but fails the curl check; a strong vortex fails both; an unguarded vortex fit folds, and the guarded fit refuses it (tests/test_diagnostics.py, tests/test_warp.py). On real data the record is qualitative ("fine warp clean, no foldover or whirlpool", sprint_notes). Each warp is checked alone; their sum in the export is not (section 5).

The microtubules catch the image's mistakes

A wrong rotation makes the microtubule match collapse, so a low first-pass match is the detector. There the microtubules re-estimate the rotation, and the result is adopted only if it matches better. In the same way, an image stretch is dropped only if rotation alone matches the microtubules clearly better. Neither step can make an interface worse by its own measure.

Measured

On a dense stack (the code names Monopoles sec01→sec02) the rescue raised the worst interface from 37% to 83% matched; its warp went from rejected to accepted and about 72% of the boundary gap closed. The scale gate corrected one outlier interface from 49% to 76% matched, its corner stretch from about 5,200 Å to 0, and left the interfaces whose stretch helps untouched (HISTORY.md). Six synthetic tests cover both directions (tests/test_warps_to_coarse.py).

Connecting is judged apart from bending

Whether two sections' microtubules are joined depends on their matches only (match fraction and shift spread), not on whether the warp passed. A warp too twisty to apply is dropped, but a clean set of matches still links the filaments; the joint check in the export then cuts joints whose two sides point different ways.

Measured

On a dense stack the worst interface went from 0 to 759 connected filaments (70 of 829 chained pairs cut by the joint check), with every healthy interface unchanged byte for byte (HISTORY.md); three tests in tests/test_qc.py. Whether the 759 links are right has not been checked.

The image fills where there are no microtubules

The microtubule warp is only pinned where microtubules are; elsewhere it extrapolates, and features in the corners were seen to jump 30 to 120 px between sections. The fill matches image patches outside the microtubule area and fits a second guarded warp, held at zero on the microtubule ends, so the two warps add without fighting.

Hint

On a real chiral stack, raising the fill's search radius from 16 to 64 px took it from about 0 to 4 of 4 interfaces filled (HISTORY.md): the fill runs, but its accuracy in the corners is not measured. Synthetic tests of the block match and the mask (tests/test_napari_stitch.py).

Seams without stairs

Two optional nudges at the seam, both on by default in run_stitch. A tangent term adds points that pull each matched, clearly tilted stub towards its partner's direction (weight 0.4), still under the warp guard. A jog cut drops near-vertical pairs whose displacement disagrees with their 8 nearest pairs by more than 2 ρ, since the direction-based joint check cannot judge them.

Hint

The tangent term lowered one interface's mean tangent break from 10.6° to 4.4° and never newly rejected a warp; the jog cut fires about 15 times across a 10-section dense stack (HISTORY.md). Nobody has checked that the cut pairs were wrong. Synthetic tests in tests/test_warp.py and tests/test_warps_to_coarse.py.

Lengths in ρ travel across voxel sizes

The microtubule gates are multiples of ρ, the median spacing of the boundary ends, so the same settings should serve any pixel size and density. The warp is fitted in ρ units too, which is what makes its smoothing ladder meaningful.

Open

Not every threshold is in ρ: the matcher's distance gate is clamped to 500–2,500 Å, and the image steps use pixels. No test varies the voxel size, and only FemalePN has a recorded pixel size (25.72 Å).

Flags instead of guesses

An interface the tool cannot vouch for should say so instead of being stitched silently: the image marks branch ties, contour disagreements and untrusted shifts for review, and the QC certificate lists its reasons.

Open

On the default path the image's review marks reach the log only, and the overall verdict is printed but changes no output. The stronger checks in the code (A–P polarity, dense-intensity verification, the image and microtubule cross-check) never run from run_stitch (section 5). The abstain logic itself is covered by synthetic tests (tests/test_coarse_fusion.py, tests/test_coarse_hybrid.py).

A fast export without visible error

The warp is smooth by construction, so the export evaluates it on a grid every 8 canvas pixels and interpolates in between instead of at every pixel. The volume is warped a few slices at a time on the GPU and streamed to disk, so the stitched volume is never held in memory.

Tested

On a synthetic 512 × 512 canvas the 8 px grid stays within a median of 0.05 px and a maximum of 0.5 px of the full evaluation (tests/test_displacement_grid_coarse.py). The speed-up (about 21 times) and the memory peak (about 3.5 GB instead of 11 GB) are stated in HISTORY.md and README.md without a recorded measurement.

Inside the blocks

Faces, ends and the length unit

Code: geometry.py (zmax_face), matching/mt_endpoints.py, _face and _endpoint_rho in pipeline/core.py.

Which faces meet. The stack must be stored with Z up: a section's top face is its high-Z end and its bottom face its low-Z end, and interface k joins the top of section k to the bottom of section k+1. The log labels each interface by the two section names, as in sec09->sec10.

Image faces. The maximum over the boundary slab, for the pose and the fill. The contour cross-check uses the mean over the same slab instead, which keeps the smooth nuclear envelope that a maximum breaks into speckle.

Ends and ρ. Per microtubule, the end inside the outer 15% of the Z range and a unit direction from its last 20% of points (at least 3). ρ is the median nearest-neighbour distance between the ends on section k's top face, typically thousands of Å, not the spacing along a traced line (about 100 Å), which would shrink every gate about 20 times.

The image coarse pose

Code: image_only_poses, _agree_rotation, _ransac_rigid and _affine_refine in image_pose.py; contour_rotation.py. Two volumes are held at a time, at half resolution.

  • Windows scale with the face. Block-match search radius: the face size divided by 8, kept between 64 and 256 px. Cell window: half-size at least 64 px or the face size divided by 16. RANSAC inlier distance: at least 12 px or the search radius divided by 6.
  • Three checks, each marking the interface for review in the log. Branch tie: the opposite branch keeps at least 85% of the winner's support; a normalised cross-correlation over a central disk (radius 0.45 of the face) then picks the branch, and the mark stays (sign). Contour disagrees: the nuclear-envelope outline and the dark organelles of the mean faces give a second angle; more than 10° from the sweep, or unsure itself, marks sign. Too few agreeing cells: under 12 cells, or under 12% of them, sets the shift to 0 but keeps the rotation, and marks shift.
  • The x/y stretch. With the angle fixed and the shift trusted, a second block match with a small window (half-size 20 px on a 1,024 px face, scaled with the face) feeds a weighted RANSAC on 3 pairs at a time (300 tries) for a full 2 × 2 map. Its stretches are clamped to 0.85–1.15 per axis and 0.90–1.10 in area, and it is committed only when the larger stretch exceeds the smaller by more than 2%, with at least 12 inliers. Otherwise the rigid pose stands.
  • From relative to absolute. Each interface's relative pose is composed onto the previous section's absolute pose; section 0 is the identity.

Matching the ends

Code: match_sections in matching/matcher.py; _bootstrap_correspondences and register_warps_to_coarse in pipeline/core.py.

  • Optional constellation term (off by default, constellation_weight=0): blends into the cost how well each pair's local neighbourhood pattern agrees (a shape-context histogram of its 10 nearest ends within 6 ρ), to separate parallel neighbours in dense bundles. Suggested 0.3–0.5.
  • Confidence for the QC. Match fraction (pairs over the smaller end count after deduplication) and shift spread (the length of the standard deviation of the pair shifts, in ρ). The rescue reads the match fraction of the first, un-bootstrapped pass.
  • Jog cut, for the graph only. After the warp is fitted, near-vertical pairs (in-plane direction under 0.2 on either side) whose displacement differs from the median of their 8 nearest pairs by more than 2 ρ are dropped from the pairs used to chain microtubules. They still shaped the warp.

The guarded warp

Code: fit_guarded_warp in transform/warp.py; FieldCertificate in transform/diagnostics.py. The same function fits the bootstrap's warps, the final MT warp and the fill warp.

map          f(x) = x + u(x),   J = I + ∇u
fold check   det J = (1 + ∂u_x/∂x)(1 + ∂u_y/∂y) − (∂u_x/∂y)(∂u_y/∂x)  ≥ ε = 0.05
swirl check  |curl u| = |∂u_y/∂x − ∂u_x/∂y|  ≤ Ω = 0.3   (run_stitch; library default 1.0)
grid         48 × 48 over the pairs' box plus 10%, central differences, border row dropped
  • Ladder. Smoothing 0 (exact through the pairs), then 1, 5, 20 and 100, in ρ units; the first that passes wins. The ladder is capped on purpose: a real fold or vortex would need to be flattened away entirely, so it is rejected instead.
  • Too few pairs. Under 4 pairs a spline cannot be fitted: the warp is the identity, which is trivially safe.
  • Tangent points. For each pair whose two directions are both clearly in-plane (length over 0.2), one more point 0.2 ρ along the moving stub's direction is mapped to the same step along a direction pulled 40% of the way towards the reference stub's. The pull is soft, so it never fully hides a wrong match.
  • Also reported, not gated: the Okubo–Weiss minimum, which is negative where rotation dominates.

The QC certificate and the joint verdict

  • Accepted means every gating check passes; otherwise the interface is flagged and the reasons are logged. The run's overall verdict is "every interface accepted".
  • Chainable needs only the two match checks, so a joint whose warp failed can still link its microtubules.
  • An interface with fewer than 2 ends on a face, or fewer than 2 pairs, is image-only: accepted, not chainable, no MT warp; the fill covers it.
  • In the terminal each joint gets one line: connected · warp applied, connected · warp too twisty — kept coarse alignment (the MT warp is not applied; the fill warp still can be), or REVIEW … not connected. The full table always goes to stitch_log.txt; --dev also prints it.

The MT rescue and the scale gate

Code: rescue_coarse_poses, _rescue_relative and gate_coarse_scale in pipeline/core.py; called from stitch_sections in pipeline/stitcher.py. They target different interfaces.

Rotation rescue. Triggered where the first, un-bootstrapped match fraction is under 0.30; the bootstrap could otherwise lift a collapsed interface above the gate and hide a wrong rotation. The microtubule ends are searched for a rotation with the multi-seed Coherent Point Drift (12 seed angles, outlier weight 0.4, seeds ranked by the median residual), and a rigid move is fitted from the matches. It is adopted only if its match fraction is at least 0.30 and higher than the image's.

Stretch recovery inside the rescue. A rescued interface has lost the image's stretch, so with at least 12 bootstrapped pairs a full 2 × 2 map is fitted to them. It is kept when the larger stretch exceeds the smaller by more than 5% and the match does not drop. On Monopoles the residual stretch is about 1% where the image already applied it, and about 10% on the rescued sec01→sec02.

Scale gate. For every interface whose relative pose stretches or shrinks by more than 5% along either axis, the bootstrapped match is scored with the full pose and with rotation alone (rotated in place about the ends' centre). If rotation alone reaches 0.30 and beats the full pose by more than 0.10, the stretch is dropped. The code's example: Monopoles sec12→sec13 with a 12% area gain that two adjacent sections cannot show.

Afterwards only the changed interfaces are matched and warped again; the others keep their first-pass result, because their relative poses did not change.

The image fill

Code: image_residual_warps in image_warp.py; block matching in match.py.

  • Faces at quarter resolution (the maximum over the 10 outer slices, read every 4th voxel). Section k+1's bottom face is resampled into section k's frame with the pose and, if accepted, the MT warp.
  • Mask. The ends of both faces, each grown by 24 px, mark where the microtubules already hold the alignment.
  • Block match outside the mask on a 16 × 16 grid of cells: window half-size 64 px, search radius 64 px, a correlation peak at least 4 standard deviations above the median of its map, located to sub-pixel precision. Metric: ncc by default; grad uses edges, mi mutual information (about 2 times slower, for contrast mismatches).
  • Fit. The matches plus every microtubule end held at zero displacement, deduplicated, go to the same guarded warp (curl bound 0.3). Fewer than 4 matches: no fill on that interface.
  • Run in parallel over cells in workers processes (the CPU count minus 2 by default); only the small faces are shared, so memory stays low.

The export

Code: export_stitched in stitch.py; accel.py (GPU); chain.py (filaments); pandorica/io/amira.py (writing).

  • Canvas. The box around every section's corners under its pose; with --trim-to-mts, the box around the posed microtubules plus 5% per side (opt-in, because a section's content outside its microtubules would be cut).
  • Warp grid. 8 canvas pixels (warp_coarse_px); 0 evaluates the spline at every pixel.
  • GPU chunk. With CUDA, as many slices as fit in half the free memory, from 1 to 64; Apple MPS reports no free memory, so it uses 4.
  • Memory. Volumes are loaded one section at a time; each warped slab is written straight to a temporary file and the files are joined into the .am, so the stitched volume is never in memory.
  • The graph. Each section's points get its pose, the same Z-blended warp and a Z offset that stacks the sections by their slice counts. Microtubules paired across chainable interfaces are joined into one filament id (union–find); each joined filament is oriented so it reads continuously, then split at any joint where the overall XY direction of the two sides differs by more than 45°; single-point filaments are dropped. The graph carries labels for checking chains in napari: SectionIdx, AtJoint, JointAngleDeg, ChainLength, NJoints, WasSplit and others.
4 · Use

Three ways to run

The mode follows the files: microtubules are used only if every section has a graph, and the image only if every section has a volume.

Volumes and graphs (default)

Every section has both.

The path drawn in section 1: the image sets the poses, the microtubules the fine warp, the image fills the rest. Writes the stitched volume, the merged graph and the log.

Graphs only

Every section has a graph, some lack a volume. Code: hybrid_coarse, register_section_stack.

  • Rotation per interface from the ends alone: Coherent Point Drift from 12 seed angles, with diagnostics for a 180° flip and a near-collinear bundle.
  • Ambiguous interfaces are resolved towards the trend of the confident ones when one branch lies within 30° of it and the other does not; otherwise they stay flagged. The A–P polarity hint needs section images, which run_stitch does not pass.
  • Rigid fit per interface, then a global least-squares solve with section 0 fixed and each interface weighted by its match fraction squared. Scale is off by default (--allow-scale, prior 1.0), smoothness 0.
  • Guarded warp and QC as above; no fill. Writes the graph and the log only.

Volumes only

Some section has no graph, every section has a volume.

  • The image coarse pose as above, then the image fill over the whole face as the only fine warp.
  • The run starts with a warning: on a round or symmetric section the image alone may give a wrong rotation, so check it by eye.
  • Writes the stitched volume and the log, plus a merged graph, without chaining, if some sections have graphs.

Running it

pip install pandorica                 # stitcher and command line
pip install "pandorica[napari]"       # plus the napari validator

pandorica stitch --input-dir path/to/sections          # writes path/to/sections/stitched_output/
pandorica stitch --input-dir path/to/sections --save-inspect --trim-to-mts --dev
  • Every argument of run_stitch is a flag: underscores become dashes, and yes-or-no settings come as pairs such as --zblend/--no-zblend. From Python: run_stitch("path/to/sections") in pandorica.stitch.cli.
  • The terminal shows one line per interface and per joint; --dev streams every detail. stitch_log.txt always holds everything, starting with the settings used, so a run can be repeated exactly.
  • --save-inspect writes stitch_inspect.npz (match lines, warp field, |curl| and QC per interface), which the napari Warp / Match Inspector overlays without running the stitch again.
  • ASCII Amira volumes parse about 200 times slower than binary ones, and the run reads each volume more than once; the run warns and suggests re-saving them as binary.

Recorded times, both on 11-section stacks: running the 12 seeds of the Coherent Point Drift search in parallel cut it from 33.2 to 7.3 s per interface; on a Mac with MPS the warp phase took 332.0 s (from 463.6 s) once first-pass results were reused (HISTORY.md). No end-to-end time of a current default run is recorded.

5 · Status

Where it stands

The review of 2026-09-23 and 24 measured the default path on three real stacks: Monopoles (10 interfaces), C. elegans FemalePN and C. elegans MaleMeiosis. Its scripts are in scratch-workspace/stitch-review/ (local, not in git); their printed results exist only in that session's record, and the data were not on this machine to measure again. No stitcher code has changed since, so the findings still describe the code.

Built

The default path, end to end (pandorica 1.5.0): image coarse pose, microtubule warp with the bootstrap, rescue and scale gate, image fill, Z-blended export on CPU, CUDA or MPS, with the merged, chained graph. 30 test files, all synthetic except three structure-only smoke tests on FemalePN.

Ruled out

Tilted section surfaces and a missing slab between sections. The fitted surface tilt is at most 0.06° per section on Monopoles, 0.15° on FemalePN and 0.12° on MaleMeiosis, so the flat 15% band misses at most 1% of the ends. On 10 Monopoles interfaces the gap estimate has a median of +21 Å (from −4 to +31 Å): small, not exactly zero, and the stub slope explains 0–2% of the residual on the interfaces recorded. Extrapolating the ends across the gap would not help.

Finding

The assignment counts pairs before quality. A pair that fails a gate gets a cost of 10 times the largest real cost plus 100 (_assign in matching/matcher.py), so one more pair always outweighs a better routing. With a price of 0.35 for leaving an end unmatched, 5.1% of the pairs (479 of 9,405) would change on Monopoles; on 7 of 10 interfaces the pairs that change sit 0.97–1.48 ρ from their neighbours' consensus now, against 0.13–0.17 ρ with the price; on 2 interfaces the price is worse. One match pass at hand-recorded poses, and "consensus" is a proxy, not ground truth. The review's best lead for improvement.

Finding

The bootstrap keeps its first guesses, and the joint split has no near-vertical guard. 92–97% of the first pass's pairs survive to the second on 3 Monopoles interfaces. split_chains_at_joints judges near-vertical microtubules by their XY direction, which is noise for them: on Monopoles 39% of near-vertical microtubules (106) have two halves more than 45° apart within one section, so real ones may be split. The jog cut is a separate check on matches and does not prevent this.

Finding

The export's inverse is first-order, and the summed warp is not checked. On Monopoles with Z-blend the volume lands within a median of 0.10–0.50 px and a 99th percentile of 1.05–3.11 px of an exact inverse, but up to 37 px on sec08→09 and sec09→10, where the exact inverse did not converge, so those maxima are unreliable. The MT and fill warps are each checked alone; their sum is not. Whether the 48 × 48 check misses steep local warps is not confirmed: the fine-grid measurement was stopped.

Not run

Checks in the code that the default path never calls. A–P polarity and the dense-intensity verification need section images, which run_stitch never passes, in any mode. The image and microtubule cross-check (reconcile_image_mt) is called only by its tests, although the README describes it as running. The image's review marks reach the log only, and the overall verdict is printed but changes no output.

Docs

Text to correct. The README's ρ points to transform/scale.py, whose rho is a mean over all traced points and is not used by the pipeline (the pipeline uses the median end spacing). HOW_IT_WORKS.md says the sweep "never gets fooled by symmetry", while HISTORY.md records a grossly wrong image rotation on a near-circular section. The matcher's docstring gives the smoothness gates as 30° and 45°; the defaults are 45° and 60°. eggshell_rotation.py is imported nowhere.

Next

Ground truth. Curated Amira Serial Section Aligner stacks (manual and checked automatic matches, plus ends marked as unmatched) would score the matcher and the rescue independently of pandorica's own errors; the file format is decoded, and the work is paused until a curated stack is chosen. Then: a "no partner" price in the assignment, a near-vertical guard in the joint split, and the warp check on the summed field. From the README: tune A–P polarity on real data, resolve flagged interfaces beyond flagging, allocate only occupied canvas tiles, couple microtubules across several gaps, load input volumes in slabs, and validate on more datasets.
